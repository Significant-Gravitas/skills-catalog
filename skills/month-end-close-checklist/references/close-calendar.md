# Close calendar

A small-business close normally takes 5 to 10 business days after month-end
[1]. Postings for multi-client bookkeepers set "books closed by day 10 or
faster" [2]. The skeleton below is a **default — confirm with the owner**; store
the agreed version in memory or `intake.json` and use it every month.

| Business day after month-end | Work | Controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1-2 | Cutoff: sources arrive; finish invoicing; check for bills not yet received | S1-S6, 2, 13 |
| 2-4 | Reconciliations: bank, card, processor, loans | 1, 10, 6 |
| 4-5 | Balance-sheet review: receivables, payables, Undeposited Funds, suspense, OBE | 4, 11, 12 |
| 5-6 | P&L variance review against prior period and plan | 8 |
| 6-7 | Adjustments proposed with documentation; accountant items packaged | 7, 9 |
| 7-10 | Final review and owner approval of the close report; then, after approval, the lock date the owner sets is recorded | 14; 15 after approval |

## Sequence notes (Bragg)

- Finish invoicing and check for supplier bills that have not arrived before
  reconciling; then reconcile; then review the draft statements. The first draft
  "nearly always" contains errors, so plan a review pass [3].
- Skip small accruals and standardise recurring ones [4]. An accrual candidate
  below the owner's stated threshold may be **listed** for the accountant, not
  proposed. With no threshold supplied, list every candidate. Qualitative
  materiality still applies whatever the size (see `controls-guide.md`).

## Late sources

A late statement delays everything after it on the critical path (statement,
then reconciliation, then accountant items, then P&L, then review). Say so in
the report with the expected date; never work around it by reconciling to a
feed or estimating the missing month. When the due date is known, offer a
follow-up check (SKILL.md step 3).
