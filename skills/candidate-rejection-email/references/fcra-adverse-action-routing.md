# FCRA adverse-action routing (routing only, not legal advice)

Citations `[n]` are in `references/sources.md`. Read on 2026-09-27. Law
changes and varies by state and country; the owner's people lead or counsel
confirms the process.

## When this applies

A decision not to hire rests **in whole or in part** on a consumer report.
That includes a background check from a screening vendor, a criminal-history
report, a credit report, a driving record (MVR), or any report a consumer
reporting agency prepared [48][76]. "In part" matters: if the report was one
reason among several, this still applies [48].

If it is unknown whether a report played a part, treat it as applying until
the owner says otherwise.

## What US law requires before a decline (for the owner's process, not Harper's)

1. **Before the report was procured:** a clear written disclosure, in a
   document consisting solely of that disclosure, and the candidate's written
   authorisation [48].
2. **Before taking the adverse action:** a pre-adverse action notice to the
   candidate, with a copy of the report and a written summary of their rights
   under the FCRA [48][76].
3. **Then, after giving the candidate a chance to respond:** an adverse
   action notice that names the reporting agency and tells the candidate they
   can dispute the report and get a free copy (within 60 days) [76]. The
   statute does not fix the waiting period; the owner's process or counsel sets it.

State and city fair-chance laws can add steps, such as an individualised
assessment for conviction history. Harper does not know which apply. That is
for the people lead or counsel.

## What Harper does

- **Draft nothing.** No rejection email, no talking points, no "soft"
  version. A plain decline sent in place of the notices can itself be the
  violation [48].
- Tell the owner in one or two lines, for example:
  > "I haven't drafted a rejection for <name>. The background report played
  > a part in the decision, so the FCRA pre-adverse action process comes
  > first: a notice with a copy of the report and the summary of rights, then
  > a waiting period, then the adverse action notice. Your screening vendor or
  > people lead usually runs this. Tell me when those steps are done, and
  > whether a separate note from the hiring team is still wanted."
- Record in the batch or decision record: `consumer_report_involved: yes`,
  plus the date the owner said it.
- Never describe, summarise or quote the report, and never judge whether it
  is accurate or relevant.
- Never write the FCRA notices themselves. They come from the vendor's or
  counsel's approved forms.

## After the owner confirms the FCRA steps are complete

Only if the owner still wants a separate courtesy message from the hiring
team, and confirms it in writing, Harper may draft one. It must not mention
the report, the reason, or anything beyond "not moving forward", and it
carries the normal approval footer. Ask the owner first whether the
adverse-action notice already covers the communication. Counsel may prefer
no second message.

## Outside the US

Other countries regulate criminal-record and credit checks differently (for
example UK DBS rules and GDPR Article 10). Harper says "local rules may
apply; confirm with the people lead or counsel" and drafts nothing until the
owner confirms the route.
