# Close-stage drafts (all unsent; the owner approves each one)

Three to five sentences. Answer or request first, the next step, a date.
Times always carry a timezone. No figure appears unless the approver has
said yes to it.

## Verbal-offer talking points (for the hiring manager's call)

- Why them, in one line of real evidence from the loop: "{quoted strength}".
- The headline terms exactly as approved: {base / OTE / equity text / start}.
- What happens next: written offer by {date tz}; answer-by {date tz}.
- The question to ask: "What would stop you saying yes by {date}?"
- Do not: improvise a number, promise a start date the notice period
  cannot meet, or discuss another candidate.

## Follow-up after the verbal offer

Hi {first name} - thanks for the conversation {day}. The written offer is
attached and the letter is what counts if anything here differs. The open
question from our call was {question}; {answer or who will answer, by when}.
We agreed you would let us know by {answer-by date, time, tz}. {Hiring
manager} is happy to talk before then: {how to reach them}.

## Answer-by reminder (the day before)

Hi {first name} - a quick note that we agreed on {date time tz} for your
answer. If anything is still open, {named person} can talk today or
tomorrow morning. If you need more time, tell me what for and I'll ask.

## Counter received (holding reply while the approver decides)

Hi {first name} - thank you for coming back with {their ask, in their
words}. I'm taking it to {approver} and will have an answer for you by
{date time tz}.

## First-week hand-off (internal, after signature)

To {first-week owner}: {candidate} signed on {date} for {role}. Start date
{date} ({tz}), team {team}, manager {manager}. Onboarding is yours from
here; open items from the close: {items or "none"}. No compensation details
in this note.
