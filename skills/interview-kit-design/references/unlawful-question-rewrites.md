# Drifting questions and their job-related rewrites

Use with `scripts/question_lint.py`: every ERROR hit points to a row here.
Rule of thumb: ask about the job's requirement, never about the person's
circumstances. `[n]` numbers follow the recruiting dossier (section 8).
Orientation only, dated 2026-09; employment-law questions go to the owner's
attorney with a one-line brief and the facts (persona).

## 1. Rewrite table

| Drifting question | Why it drifts | Job-related rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Do you have young kids?" / "Any plans to start a family?" / "Who looks after your children?" | Marital status, children and childcare questions may be treated as evidence of intent to discriminate [74] | "The role travels one week a month; does that work for you?" |
| "Are you married?" / "What does your partner do?" | Marital status [74]; sex [34] | Remove. If relocation matters: "The role is based in Porto from January; does that work?" |
| "How old are you?" / "What year did you graduate?" / "Is this a young team fit?" / "digital native" | Age and age proxies; ADEA help-wanted rules name "young", "recent graduate" and similar [45]; proxies [34] | "Tell me about the most recent project where you <did the work>." |
| "You'd be overqualified, no?" | Common age proxy [34] | "What draws you to this scope of role?" |
| "Any health issues we should know about?" / "How many sick days did you take?" / "Do you take medication?" | No disability-related questions or medical exams before an offer (ADA) [35][46]; UK: no health questions before an offer except narrow cases [58] | "Can you <essential function>, with or without reasonable accommodation?" |
| "Does heart disease run in your family?" | Genetic information includes family medical history (GINA) [47] | Remove. |
| "Which church do you go to?" / "Which holidays do you observe?" | Religion | "The role covers one weekend a month; does that work for you?" |
| "Where are you originally from?" / "Where did you grow up?" / "Is that accent Polish?" | National origin, accent [34] | Remove. If authorisation matters, see section 3. |
| "What language do you speak at home?" / "Is English your first language?" | National-origin proxy [34] | Section 4. |
| "Which clubs or societies do you belong to?" | Can reveal protected traits [34] | "Do you hold <licence the job requires>?" |
| "Have you ever been arrested?" | Jurisdiction-specific timing rules; background checks follow the owner's FCRA process [48] | Remove from interviews; route to the owner. |
| "What's your current salary?" / "What were you paid at X?" | Salary-history bans (California Labor Code 432.3 [56]; state list [75]) | Offer stage only: "What are your pay expectations for this role?" |
| "How confident are you?" / "Are you passionate about this?" / "culture fit" / "Would you fit in with our young, energetic team?" | Affect and "fit" are not job evidence; emotion inference is prohibited in EU workplaces [73]; "fit" encodes evaluator background [79] | Ask for behaviour: "Tell me about a time you pushed back on a plan; what happened?" |
| "Do you have a car?" | Can screen out by income or disability | "The role visits three client sites a week; how would you get there?" (practitioner judgement, unsourced) |

## 2. The disability pattern [35][46]

Before an offer, ask whether the candidate can perform the essential
functions **with or without reasonable accommodation**, and describe the
function, not the body: "The role lifts 20 kg boxes onto shelves several
times an hour; can you do that, with or without reasonable accommodation?"
Never ask about the condition, its cause, treatment or history.

## 3. Work authorisation

Only if the owner's process needs it, as a yes/no: "Are you legally
authorised to work in <country>?" and, if sponsorship policy is fixed,
"Will you now or in future need sponsorship for a work visa?" Never ask
citizenship, birthplace or nationality. Practitioner judgement (unsourced)
for the exact wording; the persona already restricts it to yes/no.

## 4. Language

Test a language only when the work happens in it, and test it by doing the
work: "Take five minutes to read this customer email in German and tell me
how you'd answer." Never ask what they speak at home or about an accent.

## 5. Accommodation

Every kit names who handles accommodation requests
(`accommodation_contact` in the kit). Interviewers never ask for medical
detail and never decide an accommodation themselves.
