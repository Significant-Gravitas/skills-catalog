# Contingencies: order, source and what to flag

Routing and checks only, not legal advice. Citations `[n]` are in
`references/sources.md`. Read on 2026-09-27. Rules differ by country and
state; the people lead or counsel owns the wording.

## The one rule for Harper

Contingency lines come **only from the company's approved offer template**,
in the template's order and words. Harper never writes, reorders or softens
a condition, and never adds one because the manager asked in chat. If a
wanted condition is not in the template, it goes to the people lead as an
open item.

## What to check, and flag to the owner

| Contingency | Check | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Background check or other consumer report | A standalone written disclosure (a document consisting solely of the disclosure) and the candidate's written authorisation must come **before** the report is procured. If `fcra_disclosure_on_record` is not `true`, flag it. If a report later leads to withdrawing the offer, the pre-adverse and adverse action notices apply (route as in candidate-rejection-email) | [48][76] |
| Medical exam or medical inquiry | Allowed only **after** a conditional offer, and only if every entering employee in the same job category gets it, regardless of disability. Never before the offer; never for this candidate alone | [46] |
| Drug test | Treated as medical in some places and not in others. Template wording only; flag it for the people lead | practitioner judgement (unsourced) |
| Right to work / work authorisation | Template wording only. Checks apply to every hire, not selectively by name, accent or origin | [34] |
| References | Only if the template or policy includes them, on the same job-related criteria | [24] |
| Export-control or security clearance | Template wording only; the people lead owns eligibility questions | practitioner judgement (unsourced) |

## Typical order in a letter (follow the template when it differs)

1. The offer and role.
2. Pay and benefits (approved text).
3. Conditions, in template order. Commonly: right to work; background check
   with separate consent; references if used; a post-offer medical only
   where the category rule above is met.
4. Acceptance and response-by date.
5. The "this letter controls" clause and the signatory.

## Never

- Describe tax, equity vesting, benefits eligibility, immigration or
  employment rights beyond the approved source text.
- Say that a condition is "just a formality".
- Make the offer depend on anything learned from a protected trait, a medical
  question before the offer, or pay history.
