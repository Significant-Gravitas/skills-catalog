# Drifting or unlawful questions and their job-related rewrites

Used with `scripts/question_lint.py`: each lint hit names a row (R1 to R15).
This is a routing aid, not legal advice. Laws differ by country and state;
when an owner insists on a flagged question, say it may create legal risk and
route it to the people lead or counsel. Citations `[n]` are in
`references/sources.md`. Read on 2026-09-27; the law moves, so each row is
"confirm with counsel" for the hiring jurisdiction.

Core rule: ask only about the ability to do the job, in the same words for
every candidate. If a question does not map to a rubric criterion, drop it.

| Row | Drifting question (examples) | Why it drifts | Job-related rewrite |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | "How old are you?" "When did you graduate?" "Are you a digital native?" "Where do you see yourself before retiring?" | Age and age proxies; ADEA bars age-limiting terms and age proxies such as "digital native" [34][45] | Drop, or ask about the skill: "Which of <named tools> have you used in the last role?" For a legal minimum age: "Are you at least <legal minimum> years old?" only if the owner's template requires it |
| R2 | "Where are you originally from?" "Is English your first language?" "Nice accent, where's it from?" | National origin, race, ethnicity [34] | If a language is a stated job requirement: "This role requires written <language> at <level>. Can you do that?" Otherwise drop |
| R3 | "Are you a citizen?" "What's your visa status?" | Citizenship beyond authorisation [34] | "Are you legally authorised to work in <country>?" and "Will you now or in the future require sponsorship?", yes/no, wording from the owner's template |
| R4 | "Are you married?" "Do you have kids?" "Any plans to start a family?" "Who looks after your children?" | Marital status, children and childcare questions may be treated as evidence of discriminatory intent [74] | State the real requirement for everyone: "The role needs travel 1 week in 4 and occasional evening calls. Can you meet that schedule, with or without reasonable accommodation?" Put it in the unscored "Ability requirements" section, mapped to no criterion |
| R5 | "Any health issues we should know about?" "Do you take medication?" "How many sick days did you take last year?" | ADA: no disability questions or medical exams before an offer [35][46]; UK Equality Act s.60: no health questions before an offer, with narrow exceptions [58] | "This role requires <essential function>. Can you perform it, with or without reasonable accommodation?" [35][46] |
| R6 | "Does anything run in your family?" | GINA: requesting genetic information includes family medical history [47] | Drop |
| R7 | "Which church do you go to?" "Can you work Christmas?" | Religion or observance [34] | State the schedule requirement for everyone (see R4). Accommodation requests go to the named owner |
| R8 | "What's your maiden name?" "Do you have a boyfriend?" | Sex, gender, sexual orientation [34] | Drop. For reference checks: "Have you worked under another name we should use to verify your employment?" only if the owner's process needs it |
| R9 | "Have you ever been arrested?" | Arrest records are not convictions; many places have fair-chance rules on when conviction history may be asked [34] | Drop from the interview. Conviction checks, if any, follow the owner's background-check process after the offer stage it sets |
| R10 | "What's your current salary?" "What were you paid?" | Salary-history bans, e.g. California Labor Code 432.3 bars seeking or relying on salary history [56]; other states in [75] | "What are your salary expectations for this role?" (expectations only, never history) |
| R11 | "Which clubs or societies do you belong to?" "Are you in a union?" | Memberships can reveal protected traits [34]; union questions carry separate labour-law risk | "Which professional work outside your job is relevant to <criterion>?" |
| R12 | "Can you send a photo?" "How tall are you?" | Photographs before an offer and appearance questions [34] | Drop. Physical requirements: see R5 wording |
| R13 | Scorecard lines such as "rate their confidence", "body language", "culture fit", "did they gel" | Affect is not a rubric criterion, so it is not scoreable evidence; an AI system inferring emotions in hiring is prohibited in the EU [73]; AI video scoring tracked nothing job-related [106]; "fit" encodes evaluator demographics [79] | Replace with the named, observable behaviour the rubric scores, or drop |
| R14 | "Can you work weekends / nights / travel / lift?" | Fine when it is a real, stated requirement asked of everyone; drifts when asked selectively or used to probe religion, family or disability | Keep only if the approved role states it, ask everyone the same words, and add "with or without reasonable accommodation" for physical and schedule requirements [35]. It sits in the unscored "Ability requirements" section, never on a criterion |
| R15 | "What type of discharge did you get?" | Military discharge type can reveal protected status | Drop; ask about relevant skills gained in service if a criterion needs it |

## Patterns that are allowed

- "Can you perform <essential function>, with or without reasonable
  accommodation?" [35][46]
- "Please describe or demonstrate how you would perform <job function>." [46]
- After a conditional offer, medical questions or exams only if every entrant
  in the same job category gets them [46]. That belongs to the offer stage,
  never to this skill.

## Also never in an interview plan

- A request for a photograph, date of birth or graduation year [34].
- Any search of the candidate's social media for personal life, health or
  family information [47].
- Emotion, confidence or honesty ratings from video, voice or transcripts [73].
