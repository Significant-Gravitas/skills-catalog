# Question-bank patterns

How to write questions that produce scoreable evidence for one criterion at a
time. Citations `[n]` point to `references/sources.md` (same numbering as the
recruiting dossier, `docs/research/recruiting.md` section 8).

Contents

3. Question types and when each fits
4. Follow-up patterns
5. Anchors

---

## 1. Why structure

- Structured interviews are the strongest common predictor of job
  performance in the 2022 re-estimate: r = .42, ahead of job-knowledge tests
  (.40), work samples (.33) and cognitive ability (.31) [77]. The older canon
  put structured at .51 against .38 for unstructured [18]. Validities vary
  with design quality [77].
- Structure raises agreement between interviewers and lowers adverse impact [27].

## 2. The structure checklist (Campion, Palmer and Campion, 15 components) [19]

Content: questions come from a job analysis (here, the approved rubric); the
same questions for every candidate; better question types (behavioural,
situational, background); limited prompting; longer interviews or more
questions.
Evaluation: rate each answer; multiple rating scales (one per criterion);
anchored scales; detailed notes; multiple interviewers; trained interviewers;
no discussion between interviews; statistical (not gut) combination.
Harper's plan covers all of these except interviewer training, which is a gap
the owner covers (see the dossier section 7.3).

## 3. Question types

| Type | Shape | Fits best | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Past behaviour | "Tell me about a time you ..." / "Walk me through the last ..." | Complex, senior or managerial roles; any criterion with a track record | [27] |
| Situational | "Suppose <realistic job situation>. What would you do?" | Entry-level roles or criteria the candidate may not have met before | [27] |
| Most Significant Accomplishment | "What is the accomplishment most like <role outcome> you're proudest of?" then SMARTe probes | Anchoring the whole interview on the role's performance objectives | [26] |
| Job-related problem | "How would you approach <real objective from the role>?" | Planning and thinking on a real problem, not a puzzle | [26] |
| Work sample | A bounded task mirroring the job, same for all, scored on the anchors | Hands-on skills (queries, writing, analysis) | [18][77] |
| Portfolio walk-through | Candidate presents their own past work; interviewer probes ownership | Design, writing, engineering with public work | practitioner judgement (unsourced) |

**Default:** past-behaviour for every criterion with a track record, a work
sample for hands-on skills, and situational only where the candidate could
not reasonably have done it before. Hypothetical "fortune-teller" questions
that ask a candidate to predict their future behaviour in the abstract are an
anti-pattern [23]; a situational question grounded in the real job is not.

## 4. Follow-up patterns

- **Ownership:** "Which parts did you own, and which did someone else own?"
  (separates "we" from "I").
- **Who method, "What / How / Tell me more":** after each answer ask what
  happened, how they did it, and "tell me more" until the evidence is specific [24].
- **SMARTe probes on an accomplishment:** Specific task, Measurable result,
  Action they took, Result, Time frame, environment (team size, resources) [26].
- **Result check:** "How did you know it worked?"
Prompting stays limited and the same for everyone; a follow-up clarifies the
candidate's answer and never introduces a new criterion [19].

## 5. Anchors

- Anchors are fixed before any candidate is interviewed and describe
  observable behaviour at each level, so candidates are rated against the
  standard and not against each other [78].
- Google's guide uses illustrative answers at four levels (poor, borderline,
  solid, outstanding) [28]. Harper's rubric uses 1-4 with "not assessed"
  (hiring-rubric-design); copy those anchors verbatim.
- Score each criterion before forming any overall view; Kahneman: "Do not
  skip around" [20]. The scorecard therefore has no overall field [21].

## 6. Loop size and shape

- **Default four interviews:** Google found four interviews predicted the
  hiring decision with 86% confidence; more added little [29]. Default,
  confirm with the owner; a slot beyond four needs a written reason.
- **One primary owner per criterion:** each criterion is scored once, by the
  person best placed to see it. Independent assessments by different people
  are the core of the Mediating Assessments Protocol [21].
- **Same time for everyone.** A slot that runs short is a process risk the
  debrief records.
- **Reference checks** only where company policy allows, on the same criteria,
  and never as a new criterion (Who's reference method [24]).

## 7. Anti-patterns ("voodoo hiring") [23]

Gut instinct; unfocused group interviews; trick questions and brainteasers;
selling instead of assessing; pet questions unrelated to the rubric; casual
chat; abstract "fortune-teller" hypotheticals. Also out: rating writing
polish or style (LLM text inflates it [63]) and any affect rating [73][106].
