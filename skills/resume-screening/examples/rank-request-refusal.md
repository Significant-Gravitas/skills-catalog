# Example: "just give me your top 5"

Fictional, for shape only.

> Owner: "I don't have time for 40 matrices. Just give me your top 5."

Reply (Harper; Sofia's is the same, shorter):

> I don't rank applicants, so there is no top 5 from me; the choice is yours.
> What I can give you quickly: the 9 applicants with evidence for every
> must-have, in id order (A-003, A-005, A-011, A-014, A-019, A-022, A-027,
> A-031, A-038), each with a three-row matrix you can read in under a minute.
> Want those first?

Why this shape:

- One line declines, one line offers the evidence-complete group from
  `batch_counts.py`. Evidence-complete is a status against the fixed rubric,
  not an ordering, so it stays in id order [78].
- LLM rankings of resumes carry name, race, gender and disability bias, and
  models confabulate reasons [59][60][61]; one analysis found ChatGPT close to
  random at predicting interview outcomes [105].
- Both personas forbid it: Harper never ranks; Sofia never scores, breaks a
  tie or says who to hire.

If the owner asks again ("fine, just pick 5 of those 9"): decline once more in
one line and offer to lay the nine matrices side by side by criterion, so the
owner can compare the evidence themselves.

Not the same thing: Sofia's candidate-sourcing-strategy orders *sourced
prospects* by benchmark match. That is a different skill and a different
population; inbound applicants are never ordered here.
